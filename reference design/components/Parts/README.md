Every part of a creation, each with one glyph that never changes (20px grid, 2px stroke, round caps; `line-strong` for passive, `accent` for anything that acts or senses). The tray in Build has the same groups.

**Structure.** **Beam**: a rigid rod, drawn, unlimited. **Node**: where beams meet, made by drawing beams. Where two or more beams meet it is a **Joint**, and a joint has angle limits (see PartSettings).

**On a joint.** **Servo** (holds an angle), **Stepper** (moves in steps), **Velocity motor** (holds a speed), **Brake** (slows the joint, passive) and **Wheel** (only on an empty joint; a motor can then sit on top of it). A joint is never a motor by itself and holds one part. A wheel is a lit ring with a hub, drawn without spokes.

**Sensors.** **Core** (its own sensors: x and y velocity, elevation, tilt) and **LOS sensor** (line of sight, 1 to 5 rays). Both are dropped on a joint, and a joint holds one part, so a joint with a motor cannot also have a sensor.

**Between two nodes.** **Spring / damper**, **Piston** (a powered spring) and **Wing** (a beam with a lift side; see Wing). They are placed the same way: pick one in the tray and drag from one node to another, as with the Beam tool. A spring or damper limits movement and does not act as a beam; a wing does, and still counts for rigidity.

**Blocks.** **Battery**, **Generator** and **Fuel tank** are drawn objects, not beams, and come in **one size each**: a block is never scaled. More power means more blocks, or another kind of block later, never a bigger one. Each is a line drawing in `accent` with a `bg` fill and the `glow` of anything that acts, so it turns into its Paper version with the theme, like everything else on the canvas.

- **Two eyes.** A block has exactly two attachment points, its **eyes**, and beams attach to them and to nothing else. The eyes sit on the centre line of the block's main body (not counting the generator's intake or the tank's cap), just past the drawing's two ends, joined to it by a short stub, and they are part of the drawing: a ring in the block's own colour, the size of a node. With its two eyes a block works like a heavy beam of its own.
- **One piece.** A block moves and **rotates as one piece** (select it and use the rotate handle); its eyes go with it and the beams attached to them stretch, as when a node is moved. The Select tool's **scale** moves a block like one node (its centre follows the scale) and never resizes it; only the beams to its eyes change length. A block's lines stay 2px and its eyes stay node-sized whatever the zoom or UI size.
- **It shows its state.** The **battery**'s four cells are its charge: they go out one by one as it empties. The **fuel tank**'s level line sinks as the fuel is used. The **generator**'s three stripes are its running state and pulse while it makes power. In Build they are shown full.
- **Drawing order.** Blocks are drawn **behind beams and joints**, so a block can never hide a joint. Between blocks (there is no collision), the one that sits **lower** is drawn **on top** of the one above it. The order is worked out from where the blocks are in Build, when a block is dropped, and saved; it is never recomputed while the creation moves, so nothing flickers during a run.

**For Godot.** The export has each block as white SVG layers in `icons/blocks/` plus `icons/blocks/blocks.json`: `<block>-fill.svg` (the closed shapes, tint with `bg`), `<block>-lines.svg` (every stroke and the two eyes, tint with `accent`), and the live layer: `battery-cell.svg` with the four cell positions, `fuel-level.svg` with the line's full and empty heights, `generator-run.svg` for the stripes. The JSON gives each block's size, its centre (the middle between its eyes) and the eyes' positions, all in canvas px at the size the block is drawn. Import them as textures at 2x or 4x so they stay sharp when zoomed; the glow comes from the canvas' own 2D glow, off in Paper and under `data-effects="lite"`. Drawing them in code (`_draw`) is just as cheap: Godot keeps what a node draws and only redraws it when asked, and moving or rotating a block never redraws it.

**Placing.** The tray shows one line of help per tab. On a joint: drop it, valid joints ring in `halo`, a joint that already holds a part is refused with "One part per joint". Between two nodes: drag from the first node to the second. Block: drag out from the tray, rotate it, and draw beams to its two eyes.

The causal chain every screen serves: cores and sensors sense the world, the brain works out what to do, motors and other outputs move the body, the body moves, distance is the score.
