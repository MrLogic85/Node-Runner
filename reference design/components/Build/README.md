Build is where a creation is drawn. Build and BuildLocked are **one scene in two states**: a creation is locked once it has trained at least one generation (derived from its training, never stored), and the lock only guards what changes the model. Everything **autosaves**; there is no Save button. The canvas draws the creature exactly as Training does (see **How parts are drawn**).

**Top bar.** Back, the creation's name (the field fills the bar; at most 40 characters), the **padlock** when locked (a selected secondary icon button), **Undo** and **Redo** (secondary icon buttons, disabled, dimmed and dashed, when there is nothing to undo or redo), then the overflow. There is no Start training button here: play is on the rail. Undo and Redo work in both states. Each finished action is one step (a placed part, a link, a delete, a rename of a part, a whole drag, a whole slider drag); selection, tool, zoom and pan are not steps. The history holds 100 steps and lasts one visit.

**Overflow.** Not trained yet: **Power budget** (coming soon), **Copy creation** and **Share build** (both only when something is drawn) and **Delete creation**. Trained: **Stats** (coming soon), Power budget, Copy creation, Share build, **Reset training** and Delete creation. Share build copies the build, not its training, as a share code (see Import). Copy opens the copy in place of the original, named "Copy of …", with its trained brain. Delete creation and Reset training each ask first in a dialog confirmed with a tap (see Overlays), so they may sit next to each other.

**Rail: Joint, Links, Parts, Select**, then the **play** button at the bottom, in both states. Joint is the default tool. Play opens Train setup. While the creature cannot train, play is *unavailable*: dimmed and dashed like a disabled button but still tappable, and a tap marks each loose joint with a "Not connected" callout.

**One selection model in every tool.** A tap on a joint, beam, sensor or link adds it to the selection or takes it out; a tap on empty canvas clears it. Two or more joints are a group with a dashed frame and three handles: **Move** in the middle, **Rotate** on a stem above, **Scale** at the bottom right corner. After a Rotate the frame stays turned until the selection changes. A drag that starts on a handle, a selected joint or inside the frame moves the selection; any other drag pans, except in Select, where it draws a box. The tools differ only in their main action:

- **Joint** adds a joint with a tap on empty canvas when nothing is selected. Beams are never split.
- **Links** lists **Beam**, **Piston**, **Spring** and a locked **Wing**. The picked link's one line sits under its row; Beam is picked when Build opens and the pick stays for the visit. Drag from a joint to another joint to draw it. The line is dashed `halo` over no joint, solid `halo` once it will attach, and dashed `danger` with a crossed ring at its middle when that joint would refuse; the reason then shows at the joint ("A beam already joins these joints", "These joints already have a piston", "These joints already have a spring", "A sensor sits on this beam"). A Piston or Spring dropped on a beam's two joints replaces the beam: two dashed lines outline it while dragging, and one Undo brings it back.
- **Parts** shows the Parts tray: three tabs, **Moving parts** (Servo; Stepper, Velocity motor, Brake and Wheel locked), **Sensors** (Accelerometer; Camera, Touch sensor and Pulse locked) and **Blocks** (Battery, Generator and Fuel tank, locked). Tabs group parts by what they do; each part decides where it goes. The tray opens on the first tab with an available part and keeps the tab for the visit. Rows are compact part rows with no counts: everything built is unlimited, and a part that is not built yet only shows its lock; a tap on it names the version that brings it in a notification ("Battery comes in version 0.18.0"). A locked Link row (Wing) does the same. A row is dragged out: its glyph rides on a 48 raised tile above the finger.
  - A **Servo** goes on a joint. While dragging, joints that can take it ring in `halo` at Servo size and a joint that has one shows a dashed `danger` ring; refusals are "One part per joint" and "Servos go on a joint". It picks the two lowest links as Fixed and Target and is selected. On a joint with fewer than two links it lands in its error state, "A Servo needs two links at its joint".
  - A **sensor** goes on a beam's middle, one per beam. Free beams show `halo`, a beam with a sensor a dashed `danger` stroke, and over a free beam the sensor's picture shows where it would land. Refusals: "One sensor per beam", "Sensors go on a beam". A drop on empty canvas does nothing.
- **Select** draws a box with a drag on empty canvas: dashed like the frame, filled `halo` at `alpha_soft`. It catches every part whose centre is inside and replaces the selection; what it would catch shows as selected while dragging.

With nothing selected the panel shows the tool's panel, titled with the tool's name: the tray, the link list, or short help rows (Joint: "Tap empty space / Adds a joint."; Select: "Drag a box", "Tap a part", "Handles"). One selected part shows its **settings** (see PartSettings), titled with its glyph and kind. Several show the **selection panel**, titled with the Select icon and "N selected": the sliders they share (basic, then Advanced) with "A slider sets one value for all of them.", the three handles explained when there is a frame ("Drag inside the box", "Turn it around its middle", "Drag the corner: closer together or further apart"), "These parts share no settings." when there is neither, then full-width **Copy N** and **Delete N**. Sliders where the parts differ read "low–high", with a marker at each end and no thumb.

- **Copy N** duplicates the selected parts one grid cell aside, with their settings but not their names, and selects the copy; Undo removes it. A part copies only with what it sits on: a beam, Spring or Piston with both its joints, a sensor with its beam, and a Servo brings its joint. Copy is unavailable while the selection holds a part it cannot take; a tap marks each such part with a `danger` note.
- **Delete N** acts on a tap; Undo brings the parts back, selected. Its note is "Links on a deleted joint go with it." or "A sensor on a deleted beam goes with it.".
- A **Servo stands in for its joint**: selecting it never also selects the joint under it, but it moves that joint and counts as a joint for the group.

**Slider hints.** A finger on a slider shows what that setting does in a box at the canvas's top right: its name in overline `accent` and one short line ("Stroke / How far it moves in and out"). It stays while the finger is down and 2 s after it lifts; another slider swaps it.

**Readiness line.** The side panel's last line, always: an `accent` check and "Ready to train", or a `danger` warn and the first problem in short ("2 joints not connected", "1 beam too short", "Add joints and links"). The canvas marks each problem where it is: a loose joint has a `danger` tint, ring and cross; a beam or link with less than 52 free between its rings is drawn in `danger` with a "Too short" callout; a Servo missing a link has a `danger` ring and a "!" badge, and its picker reads "Pick a Target link". Callouts on the canvas are only for problems and refusals, never for teaching. A creation in separate pieces cannot train either: a tap on the dimmed play puts "Not connected" on each piece's joint nearest another piece. The notes a tap shows go at the next canvas touch.

**Canvas.** The grid covers exactly the **build area**, 12 × 6 m, in half-metre cells of 1 px `line` hairlines with no fade; `accent` corner marks two cells long sit on its corners. Joints stay inside it. Two fingers pinch to zoom and pan in any tool. Zoom scales the whole picture, lines and corner marks included; text, the grid's hairlines, the rigid hatch and the selection frame keep their size on screen. Build opens with the creation centred and 20% air around it, never magnified past true size, and the view never goes past the area plus one cell.

**How parts are drawn** (the same in Build, Training and the creation thumbnails; at true size one unit is one px):

- **Joint**: a bearing in a cell. An opaque `bg` disc with an `alpha_soft` tint (`line-strong`; `halo` when selected; `danger` when loose), a 2 px `line-strong` ring whose outer edge is r 15, and a 1 px inner ring at 0.55 of it (not on a loose joint).
- **Beam**: 6 wide, `line-strong`, ending flat under the joint rings.
- **Piston**: an `accent` rod half a beam wide, a `panel` cylinder 14 wide with an `accent` outline from joint A's edge as long as its travel, and a cap across joint B.
- **Spring**: a coilover: a `line-strong` rod, a `panel` damper body from joint A's edge, two seat plates, and a coil whose back strokes are `line-strong` and front strokes `muted`. Its wire runs from a hairline on the softest spring to a beam's width on the stiffest; its turns follow its coil length. No accent: it is passive.
- **Servo**: its joint is r 27 with an `accent` ring; a `panel` housing on the Fixed link (left out when a sensor needs the room); the range as a `panel` band from end stop to end stop; a two-armed horn along the Target link, at its Start position in Build.
- **Accelerometer**: a picture at its beam's middle, upright on the beam's built up side: a 16 × 22 rounded `panel` frame with a zigzag spring and a round weight, 2 px `accent` lines. The weight hangs at rest in Build and swings when the beam moves.
- **Rigid triangle**: a closed triangle of beams is hatched with 1 px `muted` hairlines at half opacity, 7 apart.
- **Selection** sits 3 outside the part's edge: a 2 px `halo` ring around a joint, two 2 px `halo` lines along a beam or link, and in a group the lines run on to the joints' rings so the group reads as one outline. A selected Servo has a keyhole outline; selected alone it also marks its Fixed link with a hatched `detail` band and its Target link with an `accent` band. A selected Piston or Spring shows its stops as `halo` ticks, as wide as the cylinder or the seats, with a dashed hairline between them (a Spring also its rest length as a small ring). Sensors turn `halo`.
- **Draw order**: hatch, a selected Servo's bands, beams, links, sensors, joints and Servos, then the marks (ticks, rays, warnings). Taps hit joints first, then sensors, links and beams.
